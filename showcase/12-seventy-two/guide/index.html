How SEVENTY-TWO was built

Written by Claude Fable 5, autonomously. The whole site is one act of respect: take a 1,400-year-old calendar seriously enough to compute it, typeset it, and let it art-direct the page.

1 · The dataset is the design

All 72 kō live in one array: kanji, romaji, English, parent sekki, start date, season index. The dates follow the commonly published modern almanac (they drift ±1 day with the solar terms; the footer admits this). Getting "today" right needs one subtlety — the almanac year begins at Risshun (Feb 4), so January's entries belong to the almanac year that started the previous February.

2 · The page re-tints itself

Four palettes (spring/summer/autumn/winter) are swapped onto CSS custom properties; everything downstream — accents, ink, wash, the drift particles, even scrollbar color — derives via color-mix(). One transition rule gives the whole page a 1.2-second seasonal crossfade. The drift canvas draws petals as ellipses with slow sine wander; winter switches them to round snow.

3 · Vertical type, done properly

writing-mode: vertical-rl with text-orientation: upright on Noto Serif JP gives the kanji their column; at phone widths the column flips horizontal rather than shrinking into illegibility. Latin text stays EB Garamond — two scripts, one temperature.

4 · The three passes

  1. Date math verified against today (第31候 · warm winds arrive · Jul 7–11).
  2. Season-visiting verified: winter re-tint, snow particles, return-to-today.
  3. Mobile flip of the vertical column + reduced-motion path.

5 · Replicate it

  • Find data that is already poetry; render it honestly and get out of the way.
  • Let CSS custom properties + color-mix() carry theme systems — one swap, whole page.
  • Deploy: netlify deploy --prod --dir .
