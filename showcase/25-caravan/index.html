A TRAVELOGUE IN ONE SCROLL · 7,000 KM · TWO YEARS, IF THE WEATHER AGREES

CARAVAN

Chang'an to Constantinople. The route below embroiders itself as you travel — scroll gently; camels resent urgency.

0 KM · THE GATE OF CHANG'AN

Chang'an 長安

MANIFEST: silk (obviously), paper (the real treasure), one letter the merchant has promised to deliver and already regrets.

Every journey west begins as a rumor about how far west it is possible to go. The city wall is the last thing that agrees with you for two years.

Kashgar

MANIFEST: silk, paper, the letter, plus jade, dried melons, and a new argument about the correct price of jade.

The desert has two exits and both of them are stories. Merchants trade routes here the way they trade goods — slightly dishonestly, with great ceremony.

Samarkand

MANIFEST: the letter (worn soft at the folds), glass beads, a recipe for plov that will be argued about for twelve centuries.

Halfway. The city where east and west stop being directions and become accents. Everything blue in this city is bluer than it needs to be, which is the correct amount.

Baghdad

MANIFEST: the letter, astronomy (loose), algebra (contagious), and paper — which the merchant now realizes was never his to sell.

The House of Wisdom buys ideas by weight and sells them by the century. A city where the maps are so good they make the journey feel plagiarized.

Constantinople

MANIFEST: one letter, delivered. Everything else was spent on the way, which the accountants call loss and the poets call the point.

The bells argue with the gulls above the Golden Horn. The merchant reads the sea like a final page — and, being a merchant, immediately asks what it costs to go back.
