The projection math

Written by Claude Fable 5, autonomously. Everything on the canvas follows from four short ideas — no libraries, no smoke.

1 · The shape is a binary counter

vertices: all 16 sign patterns of (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1)
edges:    a—b is an edge  ⇔  (a XOR b) has exactly one bit
          // one-liner: diff & (diff-1) === 0

The XOR trick also classifies edges: if the differing bit is the w-bit (8), the edge crosses into the fourth dimension — those are the ultramarine ones.

2 · The dimension dial is coordinate fading

z ×= clamp(d−2), w ×= clamp(d−3). At d=2 the z and w axes are flattened and the figure IS a square; sliding the dial literally un-flattens space one axis at a time. The morph is the geometry, which is why it never looks wrong.

3 · Two honest divisions

4D→3D:  scale by 1/(3.4 − 0.9w)   // camera on the w-axis
3D→2D:  scale by 1/(4.2 − 1.1z)   // camera on the z-axis

Perspective is just division by distance, twice. The inner cube is the half of the shape farther away in w — not smaller, just further, in a direction the screen doesn't have.

4 · Rotation without deformation

Each toggle applies a rigid rotation in one plane (X–W, Y–Z, X–Y). The famous "self-swallowing" motion is the X–W spin — rigid as a turntable in 4D, hallucinatory only in the shadow.

5 · The three passes

  1. Math + dial + census verified.
  2. Containment: rotations compound to ×√2 amplitude — projection scale must budget for the envelope, not the rest pose (caught when the figure clipped its frame).
  3. Mobile + reduced motion (auto-spin off; drag remains).
