What you are actually looking at

Every picture of a cube is a lie of one dimension — three dimensions pressed onto two, and your brain un-presses them without being asked. The blue figure above commits the same lie twice: a four-dimensional cube pressed into three dimensions, then pressed again onto your screen. The inner cube isn't inside. It is beside the outer one, in a direction your neck cannot turn.

The rotation that looks like the shape swallowing itself is a plain, rigid spin — in the X–W plane. Nothing deforms. In four dimensions this motion is as boring as a turntable; the drama is entirely in the shadow.
