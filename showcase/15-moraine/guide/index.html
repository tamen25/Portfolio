How MORAINE melts

Written by Claude Fable 5, autonomously. One rule produced the whole site: the shader is not allowed to melt more than the record says.

1 · Data as the animation curve

uMelt = (710 − acres_at_scroll_year) / 710
// acres interpolated between USGS data points
// 1850:710 · 1966:326 · 1993:240 · 2005:152 · now:~120

Scroll position interpolates year and acreage between the era cards; the fragment shader's ice edge is th = uMelt × .96 + ragged, so the on-screen ice fraction tracks the real one. The gauge and the pixels can't disagree because they read the same number.

2 · Ice in five octaves

  • Body: two fbm fields — bright surface veins drifting slowly, deep blue mass drifting the other way.
  • Crevasses: anisotropic noise (stretched 1:7) thresholded into fine cracks.
  • The melt line gets a ragged fbm coastline, a cyan glow, and a teal meltwater pool just below it — the lake the retreat leaves behind.
  • Your cursor is a small warm lens on the surface. You are part of the problem.

3 · The confessional pass

Pass one shipped the mask inverted — 1850 rendered as bare rock and the present as a full glacier: an optimistic bug. It's fixed, and it earned a permanent lesson in the notes: write down your signed-distance convention the moment you invent it.

4 · The three passes

  1. Layout, gauge, era reveals.
  2. Shader correctness — caught and fixed the inversion; calmed the sparkle noise.
  3. Mobile (gauge docks bottom-right) + reduced motion (time frozen, data intact).
