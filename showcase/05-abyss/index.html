ABYSS

Below this line the planet keeps most of itself.
Scroll is depth. The bottom is 10,935 meters down.
It gets dark. You have flares.

−200 m

The sunlight ends its shift

Photosynthesis stops here. Everything below this line runs on what sinks, swims down, or makes its own light.

ENTERING THE TWILIGHT ZONE
−332 m

The deepest scuba dive

Ahmed Gabr, 2014. Twelve minutes down — nearly fifteen hours coming back up, so his blood didn't turn to foam.

−535 m

Emperor penguin, maximum

A bird. Holding its breath. Deeper than three Eiffel Towers, hunting in the dark in a dinner jacket.

−800 m

Giant squid country

Architeuthis hunts through here on eyes the size of dinner plates — the largest eyes evolution has ever built, for the dimmest light there is.

−1,000 m

The last photon gives up

No sunlight past this point. Ever. From here on, every light you see is something alive, deciding to glow.

ENTERING THE MIDNIGHT ZONE
−1,500 m

The anglerfish's porch light

She fishes with a lantern of glowing bacteria on a rod grown from her own skull. It is the only warm color for miles.

−2,250 m

Sperm whale, hunting depth

Two thousand meters on one lungful, clicking louder than a jet engine to see with sound. The squid hear it coming and cannot hide.

−2,992 m

The deepest mammal ever recorded

A Cuvier's beaked whale, tagged off California. Science still argues about how its ribcage survives folding shut.

−3,840 m

RMS Titanic

Resting on the abyssal plain since 1912, rusting one flake at a time. The iron will be gone before the shoes are.

ENTERING THE ABYSSAL ZONE
−4,300 m

Dumbo octopus

The deepest octopus we know flaps along on ear-like fins, unbothered, at pressures that would crush a submarine from the last century.

−6,000 m

The hadal zone

Named after Hades, honestly. Only trenches go deeper — cracks in the planet where the sea floor itself is being swallowed.

ENTERING THE HADAL ZONE
−8,178 m

The deepest fish ever filmed

A Mariana snailfish — pale, soft, almost transparent. At this pressure its body is mostly a gel that cannot be squeezed smaller.

−10,935 m

Challenger Deep

The floor of the planet. Three people in 1960 and 2012 spent minutes here; the pressure is a thousand atmospheres — an elephant standing on your thumbnail, everywhere, forever.
