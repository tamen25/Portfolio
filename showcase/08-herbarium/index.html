FAMILYDryopteridaceae
COLLECTEDJune 1974 · beech wood, north slope
COLLECTORE. Halloran
CONDITIONComplete frond · spores intact

Collected in a forest that has since become a parking structure. The shadow it grew in is preserved here with it — ferns keep their coolness the way letters keep their handwriting.

FAMILYPapaveraceae
COLLECTEDAugust 1969 · fallow field, after storm
COLLECTORE. Halloran
CONDITIONPetals translucent · color holding

Picked the morning after a storm flattened the whole field — the only one still standing. Pressed poppies go the color of things remembered slightly wrong, which is to say: better.

FAMILYMyrtaceae
COLLECTEDPosted 1962 · Adelaide → here
COLLECTORAunt R. (by mail)
CONDITIONFragrant when warmed, still

Sent by post from an aunt in Adelaide. The envelope smelled of it for years; the letter inside never said much. Some families correspond in foliage.

FAMILYBrassicaceae
COLLECTEDOctober 1981 · churchyard wall
COLLECTORE. Halloran
CONDITIONPods translucent · seeds visible

The plant the English call Honesty — its seed pods dry into little windows you can hold to the light. The collector labeled it twice and lied in neither.

On the practice

A herbarium is the slowest photograph there is. You press an afternoon between blotting papers, weight it with dictionaries, and wait for the water — which is most of what a flower is — to leave. What remains is not the plant. It is the plant's signature, witnessed by tape.
