HOW THE CABINET WORKS

Written by Claude Fable 5, autonomously — including the game design, the ghost catalogue, and the difficulty curve that killed its own test pilot.

1 · A REAL RESOLUTION

The game renders to a genuine 192×144 canvas — not a big canvas drawing small — and the browser upscales it with image-rendering: pixelated. Authentic chunky pixels for free, and the whole game state fits in your head: a moth, some embers, some gusts, three hearts.

2 · THE CRT IS THREE LAYERS OF CSS

  • Scanlines: a repeating 4px gradient, multiply-blended.
  • Glass: one radial highlight up-left + a vignette, with a steps(1) flicker keyframe that dips opacity for one frame every ~4s.
  • Marquee: glowing text whose animation stutters at 95% — the fluorescent tube that never quite dies.

3 · GAME FEEL IN FOUR TRICKS

  • The moth eases to the pointer (12% per frame) — lag reads as flight.
  • Two-frame wing flap on a sine; invulnerability blinks at 12Hz after a hit.
  • Collecting spawns six one-pixel sparks. Juice is cheap; skipping it is expensive.
  • Gust cadence tightens with score: max(28, 90 − score/4) frames — the difficulty curve is one line.

4 · BLEEPS FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES

No audio files: a square-wave chirp up for coins and collections, a sawtooth slide down for death. Every sound is frequency.exponentialRampToValueAtTime with different numbers.

5 · THE THREE PASSES

  1. Attract → coin-in → play verified with synthetic pointer events.
  2. Full loop verified the honest way: the automated test pilot scored 10, hit two gusts, and died — GAME OVER, hi-score persisted to localStorage.
  3. Mobile + reduced-motion (flicker and blink animations off; play stays intact).

6 · REPLICATE IT

  • Pick a tiny canvas and let the browser do the pixels.
  • One verb (fly), one resource (embers), one threat (gusts) — micro-games are haiku, not novels.
  • Deploy: netlify deploy --prod --dir .
