The network

Under one footstep of forest soil there can be kilometers of hyphae — fungal threads a single cell wide, fused into a web that links root to root. Trees plugged into it exchange sugar, water, and — the evidence increasingly suggests — information. Foresters called it underground commerce. Journalists called it the wood-wide web. The fungi, presumably, call it Tuesday.

Findings, carefully worded

  • Sugar moves. Radioactive-carbon tracing shows photosynthate traveling from tree to tree through shared fungal partners. That part is not controversial; the accounting is.
  • Warnings may travel. Some studies find that attacked plants' neighbors raise chemical defenses sooner when networks are intact. "May" is doing honest work in this sentence, and we let it.
  • The "mother tree" is a hypothesis with excellent marketing. Old, well-connected trees do sit at network hubs. Whether they "nurture" their seedlings or simply leak generously is a live debate we refuse to settle on a website.

From the lab notebook

DAY 12 — The oyster-mushroom culture has crossed the petri dish and is now attempting the label.

DAY 30 — Networked seedlings out-survived the isolated control group again. The control group has filed no complaint, being dead.

DAY 45 — Asked the network a question by wounding one leaf. Received, forty hours later, what can only be described as a shrug with chemistry in it.

The forest is not a collection of trees.
It is one argument, held slowly, in threads.
