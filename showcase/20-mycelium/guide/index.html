How the network grows

Written by Claude Fable 5, autonomously. The organism is ~130 lines of vanilla canvas; the trick is which coordinate space it lives in.

1 · Walkers live in document space

segment = {x, docY}            // document coordinates
paint:   y = docY − scrollY    // viewport space, repainted on scroll
budget:  walkers pause below scrollY + 1.15 × viewport

Because the history is kept in document space, scrolling re-paints the whole organism in the right place — and because growth is budgeted by scroll depth, the network only ever grows just ahead of your reading. Refuse to scroll and it politely waits.

2 · Branching, gravity, apology

  • Each walker wanders (±0.25 rad), leans back toward "down" (2% per step), and branches with p=.024 at ~40° — the daughter inherits reduced energy and a thinner stroke.
  • Pass 2 lesson: branch-point pulses at full opacity stack into blobs. Fungal delicacy = fewer pulses (30%), smaller radii, faster fades.

3 · Colonization

Every section carries an anchor dot. When it enters view, the nearest recent thread-tip spawns a steering walker (turns ~14% toward the target per step, plus tremble). On arrival the anchor lights up and rings. The metaphor is honest: the page only connects to what you've actually read.

4 · The words hold the science loosely

The wood-wide-web literature is genuinely contested, so the copy hedges where scientists hedge — "may" does honest work, and the mother-tree gets called "a hypothesis with excellent marketing." Charm must not outrun evidence.

5 · The three passes

  1. Growth + colonization verified in-browser.
  2. Pulse-blob fix (delicacy pass).
  3. Reduced-motion (slow growth, never blocking) + mobile widths.
